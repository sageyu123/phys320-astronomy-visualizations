What makes a disk?

Parcels are blobs of gas. When they collide, the gas shocks and radiates the collision energy away, so random and vertical motions die out, but collisions cannot change the cloud’s total angular momentum. A parcel can fall freely along the rotation axis; in the equatorial plane its angular momentum stops it at rc = l²/GM.

The result is a thin disk perpendicular to the angular momentum vector, spinning faster as it shrinks. With no net spin the same cloud collapses to a ball. Real clouds have β of a few percent; a typical cloud radius is about 0.1 pc and a disk radius about 100 AU.
